# oeksound soothe2 (version 1.3.1 installed; claim R-AUD-0014)

The dynamic resonance suppressor the artist keeps on every vocal part. From
the manual installed with the plug-in on this Mac, read 2026-11-02 (its
font is obfuscated in the PDF; the text was decoded and the spaces restored
by hand, so the quotes are to the word).

## The three controls that matter, and how Marco reasons with them

- **Depth**: the sensitivity or threshold of the processing; more depth,
  more reduction. Set to the input every time, presets included; the number
  is not dB (the graph shows the dB). Marco raises it until the graph shows
  reduction on the resonances he measured and no more, then backs off.
- **Sharpness**: how deep and narrow the cuts are. High for sharp resonant
  material (drum overheads, a whistling vocal note); low for general energy
  build-ups on a channel, a bus or a master.
- **Selectivity**: a threshold on which resonances are cut. High cuts only
  the most prominent (spot frequencies in string and wind instruments; one
  ringing note in a vocal); low cuts almost all of them, to balance uneven
  material.

## Where it sits

In mixing, per take, after the corrective EQ and before the compressor, so
the compressor does not chase the ring; the measured resonance per take
(its note, its dB over the octave) says whether depth and selectivity go up
for one spot or down for a general tame. In mastering, rarely, and only
with a bench print showing what it earned: a master-wide soothe is a
mixing move in disguise.

## Verify

The take's resonance list after the print; the delta against the dry take
at matched loudness; nothing lost above the resonances (the air band within
0.5 dB).
