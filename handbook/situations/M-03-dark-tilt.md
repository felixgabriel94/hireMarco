# A dark tilt: the top sits low against the bottom

**What to look for.** The audit's spectral tilt in dB per octave and the
presence and air bands; the same against the references, which decide what
"dark" means for this genre.

**Threshold.** A tilt steeper than -4.5 dB per octave with presence more
than 15 dB under the sub band is dark on its own; against references, a
tilt delta over 0.5 dB per octave or a presence delta under -2 dB.

**Why.** A dark master loses the vocal's presence on phones and earbuds and
reads dull beside current releases; the mastering engineer's tone move is
broad and sweet, after the corrective moves and after the glue, in half-dB
steps (master rulebook, order step 5; R-AUD-0005).

**When it applies.** The references are brighter and the darkness is across
the whole top, not one instrument. The mix engineer's intent is not a dark
record.

**When it does not.** The tilt is the genre's (the references match); the
top is only missing on one part (a mix move); the audit's harsh or sibilance
band is already high, which a lift would make worse.

**The move.** Pro-Q 3, a Tilt Shelf around 1 to 2 kHz for a whole-balance
move (R-AUD-0007), or a High Shelf from 6 to 10 kHz, gain +0.5 to +2 dB
scaled to the tilt delta (a tilt 1 dB per octave darker than the references
takes about 1 dB of tilt), Q 0.5 to 0.7. A Flat Tilt when the center must
stay as it is.

**Expected effect.** Tilt moves toward the references by the amount asked;
presence and air bands rise together; sibilance stays under its threshold.

**Verify.** The print's tilt and bands against the mix and the references;
the harsh and sibilance bands did not cross their thresholds; the bench
shows the insert earned.
